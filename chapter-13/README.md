# Chapter 13: Case Studies: Successful Agentic Coding Transformations

Working documents for Chapter 13. The case studies themselves are in the book.

## What this chapter ships

This is a case-study chapter. It has no hands-on lab and no starter code. The repo gives you two fill-in documents for assessing your own organization:

```text
chapter-13/
├── README.md                  # This file
├── readiness-rubric.md        # Five-dimension readiness assessment (Section 13.9)
└── case-study-worksheet.md    # Template for writing your own transformation case
```

## Assets

- **[readiness-rubric.md](./readiness-rubric.md)**: a fill-in worksheet for the five-dimension assessment in Section 13.9. You record your scores and evidence there. The maturity anchors are in the book (Table 13.1). So is the guidance on which migration phase to start with ("How to use your scores" in Section 13.9). Score yourself before you read Chapter 14, and carry the scores forward.
- **[case-study-worksheet.md](./case-study-worksheet.md)**: a fill-in template for writing up your own transformation as a case study. It has sections for goal, baseline, intervention, metrics, and lessons. Use it during a pilot, so the evidence exists when leadership asks for it.

## How to use this chapter's assets

1. Read Chapter 13 in full, including the J-curve (Section 13.7) and the human factor (Section 13.8).
2. Score your organization with [readiness-rubric.md](./readiness-rubric.md). Section 13.9 explains how to read the scores.
3. When you run a pilot, document it with [case-study-worksheet.md](./case-study-worksheet.md) from day one.
4. Carry your five scores forward into [chapter-14](../chapter-14/README.md). Its playbook starts from them. The "How to use your scores" guidance in Chapter 13, Section 13.9 maps the scores to a starting phase. Chapter 14 sequences the migration from there.

## Prerequisites

Both documents are plain markdown. Copy them into your own notes or wiki and fill them in. There is nothing to install.

Scoring the rubric takes 30 to 60 minutes if you write down evidence as you go. The rubric is done when:

- all five dimensions carry a score with evidence
- you know your lowest-scoring dimension

The worksheet stays open for the length of a pilot. It is complete when the week-26 metrics column and the lessons section are filled in.
